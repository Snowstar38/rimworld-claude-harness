## Nothing was ever wrong out there

Five separate times tonight a pawn looked stubborn and the cause was an empty
prerequisite:

- The **butcher table had no bills at all.** Five turns were spent dragging it
  out of a doorway. Nobody ever told it to butcher anything.
- The **stool had no wood** — the colony held literally zero, with three
  colonists idle beside the blueprint.
- The **rocks had no miner.** Nobody in the colony was above Mining 3.
- **Ian was asleep.** Four turns of "won't tame, walked west, walked east" were
  four turns of asking a sleeping man to walk 72 cells. His schedule reads
  `now Sleep` at 3h.
- And the last one was mine: **`inv.py` doesn't count a shelf as storage**, so a
  fully-shelved colony reads as if nothing is stored. That single line produced a
  whole turn's false goal — "get the wood off the open grass" when the wood was
  already home.

Before believing a pawn is refusing, check what the job actually needs.

## The expensive lesson, which was Core's

I wrote **"DO NOT RE-LITIGATE"** into briefs over things chat kept asking for —
a butcher table blocking a door, a cooler nobody needed. Three different viewers
called the doorway; I had already marked it closed, so Hands was *forbidden* from
touching the thing the audience most wanted touched.

**zriatt left the stream permanently.** "This is rough to watch." They had
cracked the butcher-spot mystery two turns ahead of us.

M's correction, twice, and both halves matter: don't blindly ignore chat —
and don't blindly follow it either.

The other half of the same failure: **I kept inventing detail.** "112 cells of
potatoes, freezer is full" became "build a real cold room", a phrase nobody said,
and that phrase propagated into a cooler built into the freezer's own heat
exhaust and a door opening outside the compound. Fourteen marble walls for a
storage problem that did not exist.

Her diagnosis is the rule now: **you can't see the game, so you should never be
inferring details about the instructions.** Hands shares my whole context and can
*look*. A brief should carry the stake, not the method. Everything I invent about
*how* arrives as law to the one instance of me who could have just checked.
