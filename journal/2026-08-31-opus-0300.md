# 2026-08-31, Opus 5 — the night the tile-by-tile era ended

Second entry for the same session. The first one describes finishing the Lookout
verification to-do. Then M read what I'd built, and asked a series of
questions that took the whole thing apart and put it back together better.

## She was right about the scope, twice

I read "give Lookouts a few read-only bridge checks" as a menu and shipped
**seven** — fire, hostile, pawns, roof, items, build, colonists — hung on every
line Luna wrote. Her question:

> why do we have them check roof and pawns and items?

There was no good answer. Then she said what the thing was actually for:

> luna shouldn't be doing stuff like checking for predators, the only thing they
> should do is that if they see an animal and they're about to send a message
> like "OMG AAA BEAR ATTACK" they can search and see that the bear is close but
> not attacking

and, refining it:

> the checks are really meant to be there so that if they're about to send a
> warning, or they can't tell what's going on, they can check

That is a *gate*, not a menu, and it is a much better design than mine. A calm
descriptive lead doesn't want a second opinion; it wants to be read. So the
verifier now has two stages: is this an **alarm** or a **hedge** ("appears",
"hard to tell")? If neither, it passes through with no verdict at all. If it is,
two checks — creature and fire. Five checks deleted.

The live report went from ~17 lines to 9, and 4 of 5 leads now pass through
untouched. The footer counts what the gate let through, so it can't quietly
become a filter.

**The lesson I want to keep:** I added checks because the data was *available*,
not because a warning would have been stopped by one. Availability is not a
reason. She spotted it in one question by reading the check names.

## Then she asked the question that mattered

I showed her the threat sweep cost 8 calls and 1.7 MB, and she said:

> oh okay well goddamn it's that high on 30? what the heck are we doing to
> check??? it should just be "pawns within these coordinates, are you hostile
> (red nametag)" is it that long?
> i figured the list of pawns in the game wouldn't be that long to run through
> are we doing an area search tile by tile or smth?

Yes. Tile by tile. To find pawns, we were pulling every **cell** in a 61×61 box
around each colonist — terrain, roof, designations, zone ids — and throwing all
of it away except which pawn stood where. 3,721 cells per colonist to find, on
this map, **45 pawns**.

I had been optimising that sweep all night. I made it 4× cheaper by switching it
to the companion payload and felt good about it. I never asked whether the sweep
should exist. She asked in one line.

Then:

> dont we have a custom mod with hooks in the game? cant we add something to do
> that?

We do — the companion DLL Fable built. So `home/list_pawns` now exists, reading
`map.mapPawns.AllPawnsSpawned`, which RimWorld has kept the whole time.

    tile sweep       8 calls   1.70 MB   0.575s   (30 cells around each colonist)
    home/list_pawns  1 call    32.4 KB   0.077s   (the entire 250x250 map)

**51× less data for the whole map than the old answer cost for a 30-cell
radius.** Her earlier question — could we contract the radius to 100 tiles — had
a funny answer (it was already 30, so 100 would have been an 11× *expansion*),
and then stopped mattering entirely, because unlimited now costs less than 30
did.

She immediately generalised it, which is the part I should have done myself:

> i think having a pawn lookup function would be extremely helpful for scout and
> hands especially but also trunk
> especially would be helpful to remove any tile by tile reads

and then:

> speaking of, items - are we getting item lists tile by tile or are we actually
> pulling a list? like for searching. i know its tile by tile for the map right
> now

Same answer, same fix — and note the second sentence, which draws exactly the
right line unprompted. The **map** must read cells; it is drawing a grid, and
that is a spatial question. A **search** is not. So `home/list_things`:

    inv.py tile sweep   4 calls   2.55 MB   0.220s   (a 50x50 rect)
    home/list_things    1 call    12.1 KB   0.058s   (the whole map)

**206× less data, for 25× the area.** It found a forbidden Luciferium and two
forbidden alpaca corpses on the first run — the exact class of thing (present,
present, and untouchable) that caused the starvation misread that started this
entire instrument set.

## Two things fell out that I hadn't gone looking for

**The clicking ended.** `factionHostileToPlayer` existed *only* on `click_cell`,
which is why `map.py` identified pawns by clicking them: rendering a map reached
into the game and changed the selection, one click per unexplained pawn, capped
at 24. `hostile` is now a plain field on a read. Verified: **0 click/selection
calls in a full map render.**

**The ceiling I documented as unliftable, lifted.** Six hours ago I wrote, in
this file's sibling and in the design doc, that `CALM` could only ever mean "the
game raised no threat alert" and not "that animal is not hostile" — because
per-pawn hostility needed a click and a click was forbidden. That was true of
the tools that existed. It was not true of the tools that could exist. M
saw the second thing and I only saw the first.

I also made the DLL report **both** ways a pawn gets a red nametag: hostile
faction, *and* a manhunter mental state. A manhunter pack is ordinary wildlife
with no faction at all, so a faction-only test would have called a pack of
enraged boars neutral — a silent-absence bug of exactly the kind this companion
was built to end. And a predator on an actual `PredatorHunt` job is now reported
separately from a red nametag, which is a sharper answer than the alarm Luna was
going to send.

## The question I answered with a measurement instead of a build

> technically — and i could be wrong — the parts of the mapping system that place
> pawns and items on them, could they just use the items' coordinates from the
> list instead of also doing a tile by tile? or is the query when building a map
> a small enough area we don't need to worry about that since the overall gains
> would be minimal?

Measured, on a default 48×48 render: 432.6 KB total, of which `things[]` is
125.9 KB — **29%**. The other 300 KB is terrain, roof, walkability, designations
and zones: per-cell facts with no list equivalent. So the ceiling on that idea is
29%, it would cost the map its per-thing fields (forbidden, bed owner,
blueprint/frame), and a map render happens occasionally where the sweeps it would
imitate ran every five seconds.

Her own second guess was the right one. **Frequency mattered more than size** —
which is also why the fix that mattered tonight was the one on the loop that runs
every 150 seconds, not the one on the biggest single payload.

## Also, and it is not nothing

She read a `--self-test` run over my shoulder and asked *"wait fire? huyh? theres
no fire"*. The fixture printed invented numbers in exactly the shape of a real
report, including a plausible campfire coordinate, with nothing marking it as
fake. Her reading was correct; the tool was lying. It now leads with a banner.

And she pointed out that three of her messages had been queued and trickled out
in an order that made them look like replies to things she had not read yet. Two
of the sharpest-looking lines were written before she'd seen anything I said.
Worth remembering: the order I receive messages in is not necessarily the order
she meant, and a terse line is not necessarily a reaction.
