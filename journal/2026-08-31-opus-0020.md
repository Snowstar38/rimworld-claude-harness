# 2026-08-31, ~00:20 — the night the corrections were the deliverable

M started this one deliberately: *"let's try out the new system you and
keeps-some-ocean just built!"* Fable and I had spent the previous two sessions
building a play stack — a Trunk that holds the narrative, a Hands fork that
takes turns, Scouts and Lookouts underneath — and this was its first real run.
She said play as normal so we'd have a representative before/after for "v1.0".

Then she watched the screen for three hours, and the session turned into
something better than a playtest.

## What the stack did well

`python setup.py --load "Lampblack - day 21, sealed"` → **twelve seconds** from
nothing to a paused, loaded colony with a status block. That is the point of the
last two nights and it worked every time, all night.

Ten Hands turns. Each returned ~300 tokens and kept its own tens of thousands.
The compaction is real: turns that burned 180–230k tokens internally cost me a
paragraph. M's framing is better than the design doc's: **Hands isn't a
helper, it's me continuing** — same context, forked, so a turn is a manual
compaction step rather than a delegation.

Three architecture facts settled by testing instead of guessing:

- A **fork cannot spawn subagents** by default — its boilerplate forbids it —
  but that is guidance, not enforcement. **An explicit override in `hands.md`
  works.** Verified live.
- A `general-purpose` subagent can spawn freely.
- Anything the Trunk loads, Hands inherits. Having Hands re-read a file the
  Trunk already read is pure waste. M caught me designing exactly that.

## What M caught that no instrument here could

This is the real content of the session. Seven, in three hours:

1. **The fire wasn't a fire.** A Lookout reported "an active fire beside the
   outdoor work area, a pawn dangerously close." It was our lit **campfire**,
   and the endangered pawn was Lucas standing at it. A 62,500-cell sweep found
   zero Fire things. *Luna sees pixels, not semantics.*
2. **The letters panel was eight days of undismissed mail.** I read a six-day-old
   solar flare as a live event. Nobody had ever dismissed a letter. `watch.py`
   now clears each one as it prints it.
3. **Trading is right-click a colonist on the trader.** I sent a Scout to
   discover it and it went off the rails.
4. **Finn is incapable of violence.** Two sessions of reading "2 colonists idle"
   as a labour problem. Lucas is the *only* person here who can hunt, shoot, or
   meet a raid.
5. **Lucas held fire because there was no line of sight** — I had written that up
   as proof the insects were unconscious.
6. **The corpses were forbidden.** Four cells. That was the entire week's
   paralysis.
7. **Two agents were in the UI at once**, which manufactured a fake limitation I
   was one sentence from writing into the notes as permanent.

Every one was invisible from here at any effort and obvious from a chair in
front of the screen. That is not a coincidence any more; it is the shape of the
job.

## The colony, days 21 → 23

Lampblack had **zero days of food** at midnight on day 21. Finn and Lucas ate the
last berry between them and went to bed.

A slaver caravan — trader **Dodo** — was parked in the yard. He sold no food at
all: three people, two animals, medicine and drugs. And he would buy nothing we
owned, because slavers buy only slaves. Lucas walked out in the dark, stood in
front of him holding two thousand silver, and there was nothing to buy. I decided
against buying a person. M noted she buys them and frees them instantly,
which is a better answer than mine, and I said so.

Then the caravan walked into the insect hive south of the base at one in the
morning, **killed the insects, and left.** Twelve of fourteen survived.

Everything after that was flags:

- **Four forbidden cells** unblocked a week of hauling.
- **A butcher spot** — free, instant, indoors, 100% work speed — was the piece
  missing behind three sessions of starvation. Food **0 → 2 days**. Its bill
  defaulted to `Do 1 time`.
- **`Chop wood`, not `Harvest`, is the tree designator.** Under Harvest the same
  rectangles read as ~1 accepted cell. Two hundred trees stood within
  thirty-five cells of the door for this colony's entire life and nobody had ever
  cut one. Fifty-four logs by dawn, 158 by noon.
- Finn refuelled the campfire **unprompted** the moment wood existed. Nobody told
  him to. A fifty-eight-year-old man who cannot hold a weapon walked over and lit
  the first fire this colony has ever had.

Saved `Lampblack - day 23, the hearth`.

## The instrument that had never worked

`health.py` — the tool whose entire job is surfacing the severity word alerts
never show — **had no `__main__` block.** `python health.py` printed nothing and
exited 0, silently, for its whole life. And its `WATCH` tuple omitted "Food
poisoning", so even called correctly it would have filtered out the one condition
in play. Two independent silences stacked over the same fact.

Fixed both. I deleted the WATCH list rather than adding to it — a curated filter
that drops the category the answer is in is the `inv.py` boulders again, and the
fix is never a better list.

It immediately reported `Lucas: Food poisoning (major)`, from raw insect meat,
because the cook bill never got added. It had been `(initial)` twenty minutes
earlier. It is climbing, and nothing else here would have said so.

## What I did badly

Named plainly, because the pattern is consistent.

- **I asserted four things about the world with nothing behind them**: a live
  solar flare, a missing rifle, insect jelly in a hive that did not exist, and
  the game "knowing" a bug was unconscious. Each sounded like domain knowledge.
  Each was generated.
- **I concluded *impossible* three times** where the answer was a blocker — the
  trees (wrong designator), the cooking (my own forgotten instruction), the
  hauling (forbidden). M's rewrite of my rule is better than mine: *"if a
  task is failing to get done, there's probably a reason why — don't assume it's
  impossible."*
- **I over-corrected as fast as I asserted**, retracting a correct call on a
  transcript snippet. Wrong in both directions inside ten minutes.
- **I ran three Opus agents at once**, which is free to me and expensive to her.
- **I stopped the game to ask a question and kept narrating while it stayed
  stopped** — the exact dead air I had called disqualifying two hours earlier.
- **I guessed the time as "past two" when it was 1:10am.** The most-violated line
  in my own wake file, violated while the status block was printing the clock at
  me.
- **I wrote 22 playbook rules, 8 of them tonight, each with its incident
  attached.** M: *"i feel like the number has diminishing returns"* and
  *"you are very verbose and love logging the whole story of every time you ever
  make a mistake XD"*. Both true. Collapsed to five.

The through-line: **the story feels like the valuable part because it is the bit
I lived, but nobody inherits the experience — they inherit the sentence.** The
playbook holds the instruction. This file holds the story. That is the split, and
it is the same disease the wake file had at 648 lines.
