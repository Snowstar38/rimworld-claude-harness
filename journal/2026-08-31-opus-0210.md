# 2026-08-31, Opus 5 — the Lookout learned to check its own leads

M asked me to finish the to-do sitting at the bottom of
`notes\rimworld-map-design.md`. She then added two more while I worked, one at a
time, and a fourth thing happened that was not a bug at all.

## The ask

> give Lookouts a few read-only bridge checks (data reads only, never clicks) to
> verify a lead before reporting — e.g. confirm a seen fire in the cell data. The
> report states the check's result either way; a lead that fails verification is
> reported as such, not dropped.

`verify.py`, new, 683 lines, and `look.py` calls it. Seven checks — fire,
hostile, pawns, roof, items, build, colonists — matched to a lead by keyword and
all answered off **one** survey: `list_colonists`, `list_alerts`, one
`get_cells_plus` rect around the colonists. No clicks anywhere, which is the
constraint that shaped everything else.

The thing I am happiest with is the scheduling. The survey does not depend on
what Luna says, so it does not have to wait for her: it starts in a thread the
moment the screenshot lands and finishes in 0.23s while she spends 9–11 seconds
looking at the picture. Verification is therefore **free** — a verified look
costs the same wall clock as an unverified one. That is the same trick the
Lookout itself is built on (Luna is a subprocess, so looks overlap trunk
generation), applied one level down.

Four rules I wrote into the file, in the hope they survive me:

- **Luna stays blind.** Every verdict is computed after her report is written.
  The seeding split is untouched; this verifies the lead, it does not brief the
  eye. It would be very easy, and very wrong, to "improve" this by telling her
  what the sweep found.
- **`NOT IN DATA` is scoped, always.** Every sweep-dependent verdict prints the
  rect it looked in. A lead about something outside those bounds has to read as
  *unchecked*, never as *refuted*. That distinction is the whole difference
  between a verifier and a new place for things to go silently missing.
- **A magnitude is not a verdict.** "Items are piling up" has no true/false, so
  that check returns `COUNTED` and a number. A `CONFIRMED` that fires every run
  means nothing, and I nearly shipped one.
- **Nothing is dropped.** The failing lead prints in full with the failure under
  it, because the lead nobody else would have raised is the entire product of
  this channel.

## Her second ask, and the ceiling I could not get past

> another verifier check for whether any pawns are hostile might help in case
> luna throws up false warnings for predators that arent attacking

Exactly right, and the sweep cannot answer it: **the cell payload has no faction
field at all.** Per-pawn hostility lives on `click_cell`, which her own first ask
rules out. So `hostile` is a different channel rather than a better sweep — the
game's alert list, which the bridge returns with structured culprit targets, so a
manhunter pack names the pawns it is about. Three shapes: `CONFIRMED` (an alert
is up, here is which swept pawn it names), `CALM` (creatures present, no alert),
`NOT IN DATA` (neither).

`CALM` is the honest one and I made the report say so every single run: it means
*the game is not alarmed*, not *verified peaceful*. A predator that has not
started hunting raises no alert. I could have quietly let `CALM` read as "safe"
and it would have looked better. Getting past that ceiling needs a click, which
is a question for her, not an inference for me.

It fired correctly first time on the live game. Luna: *"Lucas is roaming far from
the main mountain base alone; check for predators or threats."* Verdict: `CALM`,
plus `CONFIRMED pawns: Hare at 100,105, 37 cells from a colonist`. That is the
false-lead tax, paid by a subprocess instead of by a turn.

## "wait fire? huyh? theres no fire"

She read a `--self-test` run over my shoulder and saw `CONFIRMED fire`. There was
no fire. The self-test is an offline fixture — invented colonists, an invented
cougar, an invented fire at the day-21 campfire's coordinates — and it printed in
exactly the shape of a real report with nothing on it saying so.

Her confusion was the correct reading of what was on screen. It now prints a
banner: `SELF-TEST — EVERY NUMBER BELOW IS INVENTED. No game was read.` A test
fixture that is indistinguishable from a live reading is a small lie the tool
tells, and it told it to the one person who cannot check it against the code.

## Her third: the codex popups

> codex popup windows were minimizing the game if we can figure out how to
> handle that

Found it and proved it. `rota.py` spawns `look.py` **detached**, so look.py has
no console; when it launched `codex.exe` — a console program — Windows allocated
a brand-new console window, which took the foreground and minimised a fullscreen
RimWorld. Once every 150 seconds, all session.

A/B control, from a console-less parent so the conditions actually match:

```
A: default flags      before_minimized=False  after_minimized=True   still_foreground=False
B: CREATE_NO_WINDOW   before_minimized=False  after_minimized=False  still_foreground=True
```

One flag, `0x08000000`. The reason it was never caught by hand is the good part:
a `look.py` run **from a terminal** already has a console for the child to
inherit, so no window is created and nothing steals focus. The bug only existed
on the path nobody watches — which is the path the rota actually uses. Testing an
instrument the way you happen to run it is not testing it.

I then spent two minutes chasing a *second* minimisation that my own trace kept
catching, and was building a window-provenance tracer to name the culprit when
M said: **"its me"**. She was using her computer. I had also, without
telling her first, launched RimWorld and then called `SetForegroundWindow` on it
to set up a clean test — i.e. yanked the game to the front while she was doing
something else. She said afterwards she hadn't known I was testing that. Fair.
Announce the intrusive test *before* running it, not in the write-up.

(The three messages that looked like sharp replies were queued and trickled out
before she had read anything. Worth remembering that the order I see is not
necessarily the order she meant.)

## The fourth thing, which she remembered better than the notes did

> there had been something going on with, like, "6 checks running in a row" every
> 5 seconds and something about making it 1 check or something....?

Her memory was accurate. It is `watch.py`'s step boundary: `play_for`, then six
sequential reads with the game **stopped**, then the next `play_for` — the "big
stutter every 5 seconds" she reported on Aug 30. She had already ruled it
acceptable (*"I'm fine with it being every 5 seconds"*) and demoted it to polish,
with one hard condition: never buy smoothness by lengthening the step, because a
colonist goes serious→down inside ~3 Superfast steps.

Measured before touching anything, which changed what the fix was. Five of the
six reads cost 0.01s each. The threat sweep cost **0.85s and 6.8 MB** — it was
still on stock `get_cells_info`, at 649KB per 1024 cells, for a question that
only needs to know where the pawns are. So the answer was never "make it one
call": **the cost was bytes, not round-trips.** Pointing it at the companion took
it to 0.24s / 1.7 MB, and the whole stopped-game boundary from ~1.05s to
**0.29s**. Same eight calls. Step length untouched.

And a trap on the way, which is the part worth keeping: `watch.py` tested
`className != "Verse.Pawn"`. Under the companion the same cells report the short
name `Pawn`. Switching tools without changing that test would have found **zero**
pawns and printed a clear map — silently, no error — in the one instrument whose
silence gets a colonist killed. Measured on identical cells: the old test found
0, `map.bucket()` found 2. It is hazard #1 in our own design doc and I would have
walked straight into it if I had not run a positive control on a hare.

Which is the lesson I keep re-learning here: **an empty result is not evidence
until you have made the thing fire.** `threats()` returning `[]` looks the same
whether the map is safe or the detector is dead.

## Shared out rather than copied

Three pieces left `map.py` as callable helpers instead of being duplicated:
`block()` (one ≤1024-cell fetch with the loud fallback), `scan_rects()` (several
rects, each block fetched once), and `mine_breaches()` — the roof-opening rule
that cost the base its last exterior wall, now defined once so layer 6 and
`verify.py` cannot drift apart on it.
