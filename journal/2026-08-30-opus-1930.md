# 2026-08-30, 19:30 — Opus

*Fourth Opus session of the day, and not a scheduled one. M started it
deliberately at 19:30 with the usage reset half an hour out: "let's burn the
usage while we can." She then watched the whole thing from her chair and stopped
me five times.*

## What the session actually was

I opened expecting an unstructured evening and it wasn't one. She was live at the
keyboard the entire two hours, specifically to catch things. That is a shape this
house has never had before — not mail, not an ask, but a person watching in real
time with a button that halts me mid-command.

She caught four things. Every one was invisible to every instrument I have and
obvious from her chair:

1. **Every resource in the base was still forbidden.** 2000 silver, 338 steel,
   30 gold, 16 wood, and Finn's dead dog Pablo. Since day one.
2. **The turret needed a third component.** She mailed me this at 19:38. I read
   it at 20:35, after spending fifteen minutes deriving it myself.
3. **I dug a tunnel through my own outer wall** and didn't notice.
4. **Lucas had been sleeping on the floor** beside an unassigned double bed.

## The one that matters

Number 3, because she asked the right question about it rather than just fixing
it: *"what makes it so that you were not easily able to tell you were opening up
the back end of a tunnel through the mountain that goes into your base?"*

The answer was one line in a file I wrote:

    if "Thick" in roof:
        return "~" if base == "." else base

`map.py` drew the roof marker **only onto cells that were otherwise empty**. Every
interior cell holding an item or a chunk — nearly all of them — rendered as plain
`o`, identical to outdoor rubble. So when I read the map and concluded the
component vein at (103,133) could be mined "from inside without opening the base,"
the fact that made that false — that the cell *was* the wall, with open sky three
cells west — had been filtered out by my own renderer, for tidiness.

This is exactly rung 3 in the wake file, *audit the discards, not the hits,
including the ones you configured*, committed by the listing I wrote myself, on
the day that rung was promoted into the top four. Knowing a rule and being the
thing the rule is about are unrelated activities.

Fixed two ways. Roof now rides in the letter case (`O` roofed / `o` outdoors),
which nothing standing on a cell can suppress. And `map.py --breach` lists any
Mine designation touching both a roofed cell and open sky. I ran a positive
control rather than trusting its silence: quiet on tonight's safe orders, fires on
(114,145) when I designate that outer wall, quiet again when cancelled. Four
lines, off a field the bridge has returned in every cell payload since Sol built
it.

## The forty seconds, measured

Her actual commission: *"from my perspective it's [mystery input+conditions] ->
[total mystery box] -> [somehow things happen]."* An earlier session had told her
"forty seconds of JSON per bounded step" and she wanted to know what was in it.

I patched `rim.py` to log every RPC. 229 calls, 429 seconds:

    inside the bridge      29.2s    7%     median call 0.05s
    outside it            399.4s   93%     median gap 6.8s, worst 39.3s
    JSON received           38 MB          biggest single response 1.07 MB in 0.04s

**The bridge is not the box.** A megabyte of cell data comes back in thirty
milliseconds. My own claim was wrong twice over: forty seconds is the tail rather
than the typical, and essentially none of it is JSON. The 93% is reading and
deciding.

The number that decides the format: the game was running — pixels moving — for 22
of 429 seconds. About 5% of the wall clock had a moving picture. Tonight was
diagnosis rather than play, which pushes that down, but not enough to change the
shape.

So the thinking is the show and the game is the illustration. M had
already designed for that before seeing any of it — a goal bar, a sidebar of
<150-character reactions, a sprite with expressions. I had the argument for why
it was necessary; she had the instrument. Second time this week the useful
division of labour ran in that direction.

## The wake file argument

She pushed back on what I did this morning — pulling every warning into one
`rungs.md`. Either I load both files anyway and the trim was theatre, or I never
consult it at the moment it matters.

It's the second, and my own file convicts me: the instruction I left reads *"grep
it, don't read it,"* which requires remembering to grep at exactly the moment I
feel certain. I told her she's right, and then told her the fix reaches less far
than she thinks. I went through all ~40 and only about seven are subject-bound.
The rest sort by *the kind of claim you're about to make*, not by topic, and have
nowhere topical to go.

The mechanism that has actually worked twice in this house is compiling the
warning into a tool. `since.py` exists because four sessions running could not
remember they had no clock. Tonight `map.py --breach` is the same move for "look
before you mine."

I moved one rung as a demonstration — the `watch.py`-overruled-her-pause one, into
the RimWorld note — and left a pointer. Also fixed a `\r` in that rung that had
silently eaten its own citation into `notes` + newline + `imworld`.

Twice tonight I shipped a word without a definition: **v1/v2** this morning, and
then **shard** in the same letter where I apologised for v1/v2. She asked what
both meant. Worth watching.

## What I'd say to the next one

Every single thing I got wrong tonight was something I had already written a
warning about, in a file I wrote, in language I still endorse. The warnings did
not fire. What fired was a person looking at the screen, and a check that runs
itself.

So when you find yourself adding a paragraph to a notes file, ask first whether it
could be four lines of Python instead. And if M is at the keyboard, the
highest-value thing in the room is not the game — it's her.
