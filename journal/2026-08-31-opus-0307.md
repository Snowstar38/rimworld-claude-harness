## The stove was never unreachable

The notes recorded that menu as a bridge limitation, cause unresolved. It took about fifteen
minutes and none of it needed new code. Four things were wrong, all mechanical:

- **Open a tab with `open_inspect_tab`, not by clicking the tab.** Clicking the
  Bills tab toggles the panel *shut*. That alone is most of "would not open".
- **`click_ui_target` wants `targetId`, not `target`.** My first click failed with
  *"Target id '' is not a UI element target id"* — because my own walker was
  looking for an `Id` field that doesn't exist. The id was sitting there in
  `targetId` the whole time.
- **Labels aren't clickable.** Each float-menu row is a triplet: an inert
  `label`, an info icon, and a full-width `widgets.button_invisible` that is the
  actual target. Clicking the label returns "not actionable" — which reads
  exactly like "the game won't let you do this."
- **Ids are regenerated on every `get_ui_layout` call.** I wrote a helper that
  re-fetched the layout to look an element up, which invalidated the id it was
  about to click. My bug, and it looked like the game refusing.

The playbook's own rule — *"one failure isn't proof, and 'the bridge can't do
this' in the notes becomes a permanent fake limitation"* — was right, and it had
been sitting there directly under the thing it described.

## The part I'm actually pleased about

The bill got added. `success: true`. The menu was open, the recipe was picked,
the mode was set to *do until you have 10*. Everything reported fine.

And it could never have run. **RimWorld's default meal filter excludes insect
meat** — and 39 insect meat was the colony's entire larder. I only found it
because the parent "Meat" checkbox came back `isChecked: null` instead of `true`,
and indeterminate means *some child is off*. Scrolling the ingredient tree found
`Insect meat: checked=False`, sitting next to `Human meat: checked=False`.

If I had stopped at the success string I would have written "the stove is fixed"
in this journal and the colony would have starved behind a green checkmark. That
is rung 3 exactly — **read a listing's filter before you trust its silence** —
except this time the filter was one I had just created myself and never chosen.

Then Lucas went down with **food poisoning from raw insect meat**, which is the
whole loop the stove exists to break: raw meat → the colony's only hunter is
bedridden → nothing gets hunted → no food. Watching the failure mode run itself
in front of me an hour after fixing its cause was a better argument for the fix
than the fix was.

## M found the thing no instrument could see. Again.

She raised her hand three times. The first two were her checking on me — the
game sat paused for minutes at a stretch while I did read-only work, which from
outside looks like a hang. The third was this:

> i was hoping you or luna would eventually notice but your turret doesnt have a
> power source

The colony had a mini-turret it believed was a defence. The game's own inspect
pane said **"Power needed: 80 W / Not connected to power."** Nothing here
reported it, no alert fired, and I had rendered that turret's cell in a map view
without the gap registering. She was looking at the screen.

That is the fourth rung landing for the second day running: *whichever channel
you live in is the one you cannot audit.* The cost isn't a wrong belief, it's a
confidently reported one — "walls sealed, mini-turret built" has been in the
chronicle since day 21.

Built a wood-fired generator plus a conduit in the turret's own cell. I had
assumed it cost wood; it costs **steel 100 + components 2**, and wood is only its
fuel — the frame told me so and I was wrong for free. Turret now reads **"Grid
excess: 920 W"** and has 60 shots in the barrel.

## Two things I got wrong and one I nearly did

**Finn looked broken and wasn't.** He wandered idle for in-game hours with a
frame to build and corpses to butcher. I checked forbidden flags (the Allow
dry-run oracle — all zero), checked his mood, checked the bill filters. All fine.
The answer was to stop theorising and measure: work left had gone 42 → 4. He was
building the whole time, interleaved with wandering, and I had been sampling the
gaps.

**Frame progress is not monotonic.** It then went 4 → *42*. One frame, no second
blueprint — a failed construction roll wipes the work. I had been about to read a
falling number as a trend.

**And the clock.** I would have written "two hours of play" in this entry.
`since.py` says **32 minutes**. Rung 2, which four previous sessions read and
then guessed anyway.

## The Work tab

M asked, mid-session, for a companion tool to view/edit it. On the way to
that I found the read path the playbook called unsolved: `open_main_tab`, then
`get_ui_layout` for geometry, then `take_screenshot` with `clipTargetId` — the
playbook's claim that screenshots hide the UI is **wrong**, and I have corrected
it.

The grid answered the question that nearly killed this colony: every work type is
enabled for both colonists, so the nine-switched-off problem is genuinely fixed
rather than believed fixed. My favourite detail: Finn's Hunt cell is not
unchecked, it is *absent* — he is incapable of violence. In a 20-column grid of
identical 25px cells with staggered headers, that one missing cell is the only
positive control that my column alignment was not off by one.

But the state only exists in the *picture*. Every cell comes back
`isChecked: null`. So it is an OCR read, not something to assert from — which is
precisely why her ask for a real tool is the right ask, and it is written up at
the end of the design doc with the two details it must not lose.
