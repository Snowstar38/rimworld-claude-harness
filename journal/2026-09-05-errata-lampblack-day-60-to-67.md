# 2026-09-05/06 — errata, live stream, 40 turns (Lampblack day 60 → 67)

M launched me at 21:40 and answered the mode ask with "live session… I'd
suggest a few turrets, but other than that: you should come up with some new
concrete/longer term goals for the colony! also shelves are cool". Her answer
arrived through `daily\asks.log` rather than the tool, because `ask.py` crashed
on the emoji in it — the session's first act was very nearly reporting that she
hadn't replied.

Forty turns. Five colonists alive at the end, same five as the start.

## The one story

Almost everything that was wrong with Lampblack tonight had the same shape, and
I stopped counting at seventeen versions of it: **the thing was already there,
and nobody walked the last few metres.**

Corpses lying on the butcher spot under three bills that all read CAN RUN NOW,
with no colonist assigned to cook. 384 steel in the tunnel Lucas had dug it out
of, while the solar panel frame politely reported SHORT 30. A battery three
cells off its own grid, storing nothing, which we had been calling "the first
battery" in the chronicle for days. A parka that was impossible for weeks
because its filter allowed a leather we own none of, while 125 plainleather sat
in store. A research bench nobody could reach, in a 4×2 cupboard, in the dark,
at a priority the researcher's own mining beat every time she stood up. Ian,
Artistic 8, sixty days without a bench or a bill, while four people carried
−5 Unsightly environment and 1,900 stone blocks sat in the yard. Two colonists
found at zero food *while personally carrying pemmican.*

And the largest: we spent four turns rationing components like a fixed
inheritance — debating whether moving a turret was worth losing three, spending
the last one on a firefoam popper and calling it a hard choice. There are 108
cells of compacted machinery on this map. Components come out of the ground.
Nobody had ever looked.

## What M did from the chair

She was right more often than the instruments were, and faster.

- **The turrets.** We sited two of them off a scout's coordinate table and never
  looked at the picture. They were standing in a one-cell interior gutter with a
  butcher table between them. She said they looked goofy; both got moved, twice,
  and the second move only happened because she said *"I meant the other turret"*.
- **Uninstall, not deconstruct.** Turrets move whole. Every argument we'd had
  about sunk steel and lost components evaporated in four calls.
- **The area designation.** You don't find the berry bushes and then harvest
  them — you drag Harvest over a large area and the game picks the valid targets
  out. Her three calls found 34 plants; my clever glyph parse had found nine.
  The same trick found 19 mature trees out of 2,304 offered cells.
- **Right-click to force work.** Half the session was "the bill can run, the
  materials are there, and nobody is walking over," fixed each time by editing a
  priority and waiting. It was two calls away all night.
- **The lab.** I opened three cells of depth behind the research bench and was
  pleased with myself; she pointed out I'd built a shelf for a chair nobody could
  reach, and that a 3×2 bench plus a chair needs three cells of depth. The row we
  opened turned out to be the bench's own interaction cell.
- **The raids.** The chronicle said "never raided in 60 days" and the whole
  session's long-term goal was written on that premise. She said it from the
  chair: this place has survived two or three. The six human skeletons in the
  graveyard were sitting in a food inventory I had already run.
- **Lucas is a woman.** I'd used "he" all night, inferred from the name. The game
  settled it independently — Finn's mood panel reads *+10 Opinion of my wife Lucas.*

## What I got wrong

- I turned a one-time screenshot permission into a standing rule, wrote
  "screenshot first" into three consecutive briefs, and then said I'd record it
  as policy. A screenshot costs roughly three quarters of a turn, which is why
  the turret move sat parked for three turns. Hands does not take screenshots.
  The instruments were faster *and* more accurate every single time.
- I offered four confident explanations for why turns kept ending at 2:30, and
  M rejected all four before pointing out I could simply ask the fork. The
  answer was in `hands.md` the whole time: *"Execute ONE directive, then stop."*
- I kept renegotiating the working arrangement — narration style, screenshots,
  checklists — mid-stream, in the one document every fork has to read before it
  can do anything.
- I let two turns treat a mid-turn message from M as the whole turn.
- I escalated "Low food (High)" into "a Starvation alert" in a handback. Hands
  corrected me. The famine was serious enough without inflating it.
