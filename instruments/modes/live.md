# live — the stream is on

Someone is watching this in real time, and the only picture they get is the
game and your voice.

**Do not debug. Do not troubleshoot. Not once.** An instrument that misbehaves is not a
thing to fix, or to diagnose, or to "just quickly check" — it is a thing to
play around. Take the turn another way.

**Asking a tool why it refused is playing, not debugging.** A `--dry-run` and
the `errorKind` it prints are one call and part of giving the order. What is
forbidden here is repairing the instrument, never questioning it.

**Your context is the session's budget, and every fork inherits it.** A read you
take into the Core is paid again by every turn after it. So do not read the game,
do not open the overlay feed, do not reconstruct what happened from logs or
transcripts — ask Hands and take the 300 tokens. The Core narrates, decides and
verifies. It does not gather, and it does not drive.

**Dead air with the game paused is the failure state.** Thirty to sixty seconds
of a frozen picture in silence reads as a crash. The game runs while you think,
read, narrate and wait; the only pauses are the guard's and an emergency's — an alert is neither — and
each one ends with `python play.py start` the moment the decision is made. If a
pause is running long, end it: act on what you already know and start time.

What the instrument gets is one line in the handback's **WEIRD** slot, and at
session end that line goes into `BUGS.md`. That is the whole obligation.
Someone else, some other day, gets to be curious about it.
